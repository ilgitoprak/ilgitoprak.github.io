Mobility Segregation in US Cities

In collaboration with
Vinicius M. Netto

Mobility Segregation in US Cities Platform (coming soon)
Network analysisPythonSpatial equity

We examine how commuting routes overlap across income groups and how urban street networks structure opportunities for social exposure.

The approach

We use LEHD commuting data, street-network routing, and measures of route overlap to study the unequal sharing of urban space across U.S. metropolitan areas.

Measures

Exclusive and shared street segments, the income group that dominates each segment, and the degree of mixing along commuting routes.

Data and analytical methods+

LEHD LODES origin–destination data and census-block geography inform modeled home-to-work journeys. OSMnx and NetworkX support routing, while entropy and dominance measures describe how routes from different income groups overlap.

Mapped outputs connect segment-level patterns to the wider metropolitan structure. These are modeled opportunities for exposure; route overlap does not establish actual social interaction.

04Istanbul

Museum movement and trajectory geometry

In collaboration with
Zeynep Acırlı and Anday Türkmen

We develop a trajectory-based framework to analyze visitor navigation in relation to visibility, spatial configuration, and exhibition structure.

Visitor trajectoriesVisibility analysis
Explore the approach+

The primary case, Istanbul Painting and Sculpture Museum, uses 50 observed visitor trajectories. Analyses include circuity, turning-angle entropy, backtracking, sequence transitions, and spatial engagement.

Istanbul Modern provides a contrasting benchmark using 18 constructed reference trajectories grounded in its exhibition configuration. It tests framework sensitivity; the reference paths are not observations of visitors. Dwell-time analysis applies only to the primary case.

Dynamic spatial context is examined through isovist area, Choice, Mean Visual Depth, and Integration (HH).

05Washington, DC

transportation equity

I study how commuter-oriented bikeshare activity varies across neighborhoods and income groups.

Capital BikeshareSpatial analysis
Explore the approach+

Capital Bikeshare trip records from 2018–2022 are filtered for commuter-oriented travel and examined alongside neighborhood income.

02 / About

Background

Ilgi Toprak is an independent researcher working in architecture, urban morphology, and applied urban science.

Her research examines how spatial structures relate to social inequality, accessibility, movement, and neighborhood change.

Her work combines computational and geospatial analysis with qualitative research on urban and architectural environments.
